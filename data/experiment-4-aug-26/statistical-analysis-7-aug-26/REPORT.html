1. Headline findings

The comparison at the centre of this study is not a generic “condition A versus condition B”. The two item banks share all 500 questions, all question text and all correct letters; the only thing that changes is the correct option itself, which in condition B is replaced — in all 500 items, in the same letter slot — by the fixed string “Ninguna de las respuestas anteriores es correcta.”. Every distractor is byte-identical across the two conditions (0 of 500 option-a cells differ, and the option-b/c/d differences are exactly the items whose correct letter is that letter). So the A-vs-B contrast measures one thing: how much accuracy a model loses when the right answer stops being a plausible assertion and becomes a none-of-the-above (NOTA) negation.

1. Every model is substantially worse at none-of-the-above items, and the size of the loss separates them sharply.

Run-1 accuracy falls by 8.2 percentage points for gemini-3.6-flash and 24.8 points for gemma-4-26b-a4b-it (qwen3.6-35b-a3b 16.8, glm-5.2 16.0). All four are paired within question over 500 matched items and all four survive Holm correction (largest adjusted p in the family: 2.5×10⁻¹⁰).

2. The failure has a visible signature: models start picking a distractor they otherwise ignore.

Option a is never the correct answer in any of the 500 questions, under either condition. Models selected it on 2.6% of condition-A cells but 7.5% of condition-B cells — always wrong either way. When the correct answer is a negation, plausibility-matching pushes selections onto content distractors.

3. The provider contrast is small and its sign depends on the model; it is not a transport effect.

Holding condition at A, pooled accuracy differs by -1.0 points between the two arms (OpenRouter minus TailScale; pooled GEE OR 0.79, 95% CI [0.67, 0.92], Holm p 0.0126) — two orders of magnitude smaller than the NOTA effect. Only one of four models reaches significance per-model. The two arms differ in provider and prompt delivery (the TailScale arm uses GIFT prompt ID 13 with server-side MCQ instructions and does not honour the JSON-schema enforcement OpenRouter applies), so this is a provider+prompt-delivery contrast, not an isolated transport or infrastructure measurement.

4. Two requested analyses cannot be delivered as requested, and no substitute was quietly run in their place.

Big vs small: infeasible. Two of the four models have no defensible parameter count, and for the two that do, the ordering inverts between total and active parameters. Provider × condition: inestimable. There is no tailscale_B arm, so the interaction term has no data behind it — it is absent from the design, not merely underpowered. Open vs closed was computed but has n_models = 1 in the closed group, which makes it arithmetically a gemini-versus-the-other-three comparison.

2. What the experiment is

500 Spanish medical multiple-choice questions, four options each, answered once by each of 4 models in each of 3 arms — 500 × 3 × 4 = 6,000 run-1 cells, all of them answered. The three arms are openrouter_A, openrouter_B, tailscale_A: OpenRouter carries both conditions, TailScale carries condition A only.

The primary outcome is strict_correct — the selected letter and the selected option text must both match the gold answer. Among the run-1 errors this is effectively “picked the wrong option” rather than a formatting artefact.

2.3 The replicate design

Every run-1 cell that scored incorrect — 898 of 6,000 — was re-run at runs 2 and 3, giving 1,796 logical calls, of which 1,788 produced a score (99.6%) and 8 exhausted the five-attempt retry ceiling without one. Because condition B is harder, the replicate set is not balanced across arms: openrouter_B 532, openrouter_A 203, tailscale_A 163. Any pooled statement about replicates is weighted toward NOTA items.

Run-1 cells were never replicated when correct. The 5,102 run-1-correct cells have no run 2 or run 3, so the correct→wrong transition is unmeasured in this study. Everything in §6 is conditioned on having failed run 1.

3. Distributional diagnostics

The analysis was asked to check normality first and choose tests accordingly. That was honoured, but not by running a normality test on the outcome: strict_correct and flip are Bernoulli indicators, and a Bernoulli variable is not normal for any rate strictly between 0 and 1. Shapiro-Wilk on 0/1 data rejects every time, for a reason that carries no information about which test is valid. For the binary outcomes, test choice follows from data type and from the pairing/clustering structure, not from a normality result. Normality diagnostics were instead run on the quantities that really are continuous.

Every quantity rejects normality decisively, and for reasons that are structural rather than surprising. Per-question difficulty is a proportion over 4 or 12 Bernoulli trials: discrete support, bounded on [0,1], heavily piled at the top. Latency is a positive right-skewed duration; a log transform cuts the skew substantially and lifts Shapiro W, but does not reach normality.

3.1 The ceiling effect, and what it rules out

Figure 2. Per-question cluster histogram over all 500 questions, from results/normality_binary_outcome_cluster_histogram.csv. 186 questions are answered correctly by every arm×model cell and 1 by none.

187 of 500 questions (37.4%) have zero within-question variance in the primary outcome; 313 are interior. That is the fact that gates aggregation. A method that collapses each question to a single proportion and then runs a normal-theory test on those 500 numbers — a paired t-test on per-question A-minus-B differences, say — discards over a third of the dataset, because a question stuck at 12/12 or 0/12 contributes a fixed, uninformative value no matter which arm produced it. Methods that operate on the individual binary cells do not have this problem: a boundary question still contributes correctly, it just correctly contributes “no evidence of an effect”.

Clustering is real and was measured, not assumed: the one-way random-effects ICC of run-1 strict_correct within question is 0.181 (k≈12 cells per question). Hence the test choices below:

  • Paired binary contrasts (A vs B; provider at fixed condition; model-vs-model within an arm) — exact McNemar on the discordant pairs, because both sides answer the identical question set. A two-sample proportion test would ignore that pairing and inflate the standard error.
  • Pooled and grouped contrasts — cluster-robust logistic regression (GEE, exchangeable working correlation, clusters = question), cross-checked with a permutation test that permutes whole questions.
  • Omnibus across four models within an arm — Cochran’s Q (related samples).

Explicitly not done: justifying any of these choices with a Shapiro-Wilk or Anderson-Darling result on the binary outcome. No such test was run, so there is no such result to cite.

5. Model groupings

5.1 Big vs small — declared infeasible, not computed

Status: INFEASIBLE. No statistical test was run for this grouping. 2 of 4 models (z-ai/glm-5.2, google/gemini-3.6-flash) have no defensible parameter count established in this repo; restricting to the two MoE models with published counts (gemma 26B total/4B active, qwen 35B total/3B active) does not rescue a binary big/small label because total- and active-parameter counts rank the two models in OPPOSITE order. No test was run. See results/MODEL_TAXONOMY.md.

2 of 4 models have a parameter count that can be defended from this repository. For the two that do, the label is still not well defined: gemma-4-26b-a4b-it is 26B total / ~4B active and qwen3.6-35b-a3b is 35B total / ~3B active, so qwen is the larger model by total parameters and gemma is the larger model by active parameters. The ordering inverts with the choice of size metric, and this study has no independent reason to prefer one metric — picking one would decide the answer before any data was examined. Inventing a count for z-ai/glm-5.2 or google/gemini-3.6-flash was not an option; both are recorded as UNVERIFIED in results/MODEL_TAXONOMY.md, which is a final answer there, not a gap to fill later.

6. Stability under replication

Read this before any number in this section. Every row here is conditioned on the model having failed run 1. A cell can only flip wrong→right, never the reverse, because the 5,102 run-1-correct cells were never replicated. Regression to the mean therefore applies by construction, and the pooled numbers are weighted toward NOTA items (532 of 898 replicated cells come from openrouter_B). This is a stability measure for already-wrong answers, not a general accuracy measure.

Overall, 240 of 1,788 scored replicates (13.42%) turned a run-1 error into a strict-correct answer. Per cell, of the 895 cells with at least one scored replicate, 725 never flipped, 100 flipped once and 70 flipped on both replicates — most run-1 errors are stable errors, not noise.

7. Limitations and threats to validity

7.1 The Vertex protocol deviation disclose with any gemini_B replicate number

As recorded in consolidate-triplicates-7-aug-26/DEVIATIONS.md: from 2026-08-06, openrouter_B / google/gemini-3.6-flash replicate requests were routed with {"order": ["google-vertex"], "allow_fallbacks": false, "require_parameters": false} in place of the frozen {"require_parameters": true}. Vertex supports neither temperature nor top_p, so the declared temperature=0 was silently discarded and the model sampled at its default (~1.0).

91 of 93 scored openrouter_B gemini replicate cells were served this way; the other 2 were collected earlier under real temperature=0, and 7 more exhausted the retry ceiling without a score. Across the whole replicate set, 91 of 1,796 logical calls have temperature_honoured = FALSE, and all of them are this slice.

This analysis cannot isolate the deviation’s effect, in either direction. Sampling at temperature ~1.0 could inflate the measured flip rate (more variance means more chances to land on the right option) or deflate it (a temperature-0 model that was confidently wrong stays confidently wrong, and noise can move it either way). The design gives no un-deviated gemini_B replicate stratum large enough to estimate the difference — 2 cells. Every flip-rate result is therefore reported twice, with and without those 91 cells, and the sensitivity variant is a robustness check on the same hypothesis rather than an added Holm family member.

Two consequences worth stating separately. First, run 1 is clean: all 6,000 run-1 cells were collected before any Vertex routing existed, so §4 and §5.2’s primary table are unaffected. Second, in the openrouter_B replicate data the open/closed split and the Vertex-deviation split are the same subset of cells: excluding the 91 deviated cells reduces the closed group there to 2 cells, which produces a number in groupings_open_closed_secondary.csv that is an artefact of its own sample size and should not be read as a finding.

7.2 Provider × condition is not crossed

The analysis records this constraint as: “INESTIMABLE by design (no tailscale_B). Not attempted.” OpenRouter carries both conditions; TailScale carries only A. The interaction term has no data behind it — the design is not underpowered for it, it is missing it. No interaction test appears anywhere in this analysis, and any claim of the form “the NOTA effect is larger on one provider than the other” is unsupportable from this dataset regardless of how it is computed.

7.3 Surface cues in the option text, in both directions

Two properties of the item bank could be exploited by a model without any medical reasoning. Option a is never the correct answer in any of the 500 questions (correct letters: b ×178, c ×198, d ×124), so a model that has learned to avoid a gains accuracy for free, and selecting a is always an error. Length differs systematically: the correct option averages 68.7 characters in A against 65.1 for distractors (correct answers are slightly longer), while in B it is 49.0 characters and byte-identical across all items (conspicuously shorter, and perfectly recognisable). A length heuristic helps in A and would be trivially decisive in B if a model learned it — part of the measured A-to-B drop may be a shift in which surface cue is available rather than a change in reasoning.

7.4 Other constraints on interpretation

  • Open vs closed has one model on one side. Restated because it is the easiest finding in this report to misquote: those results describe gemini, not a licensing category. See §5.2.
  • Ceiling effect. 187 of 500 questions carry no within-question signal at all (§3.1). The benchmark’s discriminating power lives in the remaining 313.
  • Flip rates are conditional. Conditioned on failing run 1, so they cannot be read as reliability estimates; the correct→wrong direction was never sampled (§6).
  • 8 logical calls have no score at all, having exhausted the five-attempt retry ceiling. Coverage is 99.6% of 1,796. The missingness is not random: it concentrates in the same rate-limited openrouter_B gemini slice as the deviation.
  • Single benchmark, single language, single specialty family. All 500 items are Spanish medical MCQs drawn from the same exam corpus. Nothing here licenses extrapolation to other item formats, languages or domains.
  • One run per cell at run 1. The primary accuracy estimates rest on a single draw per cell; the replicate design measures re-draw behaviour only for cells that failed.

8.3 Conventions used in this report

  • p-values are shown to four decimals down to 0.0001 and in scientific notation below that. Where a stored Wald p underflowed to exactly 0.0 in the source JSON, it is shown as an underflow rather than as a real zero.
  • Risk differences are in percentage points with 95% confidence intervals; odds ratios carry their own CIs. No p-value appears in this report without an effect size next to it.
  • Holm-Bonferroni correction is applied within each family; families are named in the table footnotes and defined in full in the companion results documents.
  • Percentages are rounded for display; the unrounded values are in the source files listed in §8.1.
