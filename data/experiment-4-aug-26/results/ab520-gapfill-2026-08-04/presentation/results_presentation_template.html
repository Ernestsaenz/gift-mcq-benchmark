Clinical interpretation

Answer robustness mattered more than the nominal RAG gain

01 · Robustness

Same stem and distractors; keyed-answer form altered

Accuracy fell by {{ "%.1f"|format(-result.openrouter_a_vs_b.risk_difference * 100) }} points under condition B. MCQ accuracy was sensitive to option framing in this benchmark; source verification remains essential.

02 · Retrieval

Promising signal, incomplete identification

GIFT/RAG A was {{ "%.1f"|format(result.openrouter_a_vs_gift_a.risk_difference * 100) }} points higher on matched scored cells, but the extreme missing-data bounds crossed zero and no GIFT/RAG B arm exists.

03 · Deployment

Benchmark performance is not clinical safety

These results measure strict MCQ accuracy—not calibration, diagnostic utility, patient outcomes, or safe autonomous use. Clinician oversight remains necessary.

Bottom line: report the paired A–B effect as the primary result; treat cross-platform and model-taxonomy rankings as exploratory, coverage-sensitive associations.
